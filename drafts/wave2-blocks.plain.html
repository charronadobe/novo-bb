How hunger starts

Before meals, hormones like ghrelin rise and prompt you to eat.

How fullness sets in

After eating, satiety hormones increase and tell your brain you've had enough.

Why you still eat when you’re full

Eating isn’t always about hunger. Environmental cues and reward pathways in the brain can drive cravings and persistent food thoughts, even on a full stomach.

cards-text (blue)

Structure your eating

Eating at consistent times each day can help regulate your appetite and reduce unplanned snacking.

Evidence-based obesity treatment

Lifestyle changes are an important part of weight management, but they may not be enough on their own.

If persistent food thoughts are affecting your daily life, speak with a healthcare professional about evidence-based treatment options that may help.

cards-text (white, icons)

Keep your strength

Most approaches to losing weight can cost you muscle; up to 25% of what you lose.

Quality weight loss is different. It focuses on fat loss, so you stay strong.

Make it last

Over 80% of people regain weight within five years.

Quality weight loss is designed to break that cycle.

A quieter mind

Constant thoughts about food affect nearly 60% of people living with obesity.

They're not a lack of discipline. They're a recognised symptom.

The hormones behind your hunger

Your body uses several hormones to regulate appetite.

Ghrelin

Often called the hunger hormone, ghrelin is produced primarily in the stomach. It rises before meals and stimulates appetite.

Leptin

Helps signal fullness by reflecting the body’s energy stores.
